Gravel types compared: which one fits your project?

Pea gravel, crushed stone, river rock and sand are not interchangeable. The right choice depends on whether vehicles use the surface, how the site drains, how much maintenance you accept and what your local yard stocks.

By Pro Gravel Calculator · Reviewed November 1, 2026 · Method & sources

About the prices: the U.S. figures on this page come from GoodGravel's October 2026 edition, which read 10,101 published listings from 522 supply yards in 47 states. They are material-only asking prices, before delivery, not customer invoices. A local quote can differ substantially.

1. Pea gravel

Pea gravel is small, naturally rounded stone, usually between 3/8 and 3/4 inch. It is comfortable underfoot, drains freely and comes in tan, grey, white and mixed earth tones. The rounded shape is also its weakness: it does not lock together under a wheel.

Best for

  • Garden paths and walkways
  • Play areas and tree rings
  • Decorative beds between stepping stones
  • Areas with edging and no vehicle traffic

Watch out for

  • It shifts under tyres and on slopes
  • It needs a rigid edge to stay contained
  • Loose stones can be difficult for wheelchairs and strollers
  • It can be displaced by heavy rain without restraint

Price note: the October 2026 median was $47 per ton, with the middle half of listings between $34 and $66. At 105 lb/ft³, that works out to roughly $67 per cubic yard of material before delivery. Pea gravel is not automatically cheaper than crushed stone; local supply decides.

2. Crushed stone

Crushed stone is mechanically broken rock with angular faces. When it is spread in lifts and compacted, the edges interlock and create a stable surface. “Crushed stone” covers many grades, from #57 stone for drainage to crusher run with fines that compacts into a dense base. Ask for the gradation, not just the name.

Best for

  • Residential driveways and parking areas
  • Base layers under pavers, slabs and paths
  • Drainage and backfill where the grade suits
  • Surfaces that need to carry vehicle loads

Watch out for

  • Sharp edges are uncomfortable for bare feet
  • Dust and fines can be noticeable in dry weather
  • It needs compaction in lifts, not one deep tip
  • Poor drainage or a soft subgrade still needs a proper build-up

Price note: the October 2026 median was $31 per ton, with the middle half between $20 and $48. At 100 lb/ft³, that is roughly $42 per cubic yard before delivery. A driveway also needs a base course, so the surface stone is only one line in the quote.

3. River rock

River rock is naturally rounded and usually larger than pea gravel, often 1 to 5 inches across. It is heavy, decorative and expensive relative to crushed stone. The smooth, rounded shape makes it a poor load-bearing surface, but it works well where appearance and erosion protection matter more than traffic.

Best for

  • Dry creek beds and decorative borders
  • Downspout outlets and drainage features
  • Garden accents and around water features
  • Slope protection where a local design allows it

Watch out for

  • It rolls under vehicle traffic
  • It is heavy and awkward to move by hand
  • Large gaps trap leaves and debris
  • It is the most expensive material in this comparison

Price note: the October 2026 median was $106 per ton, with the middle half between $65 and $177. At 108 lb/ft³, that is roughly $155 per cubic yard before delivery. The wide range reflects size, colour, region and how far the stone travels.

4. Sand

Masonry sand is a fine aggregate used for paver bedding, joint sand and some drainage details. It is not a substitute for a compacted gravel base, and it should not be used as a driveway surface. For play areas, specify washed sand rather than masonry sand.

Price note: the October 2026 median for masonry sand was $48 per ton, with the middle half between $34 and $60. At 110 lb/ft³, that is roughly $71 per cubic yard before delivery. The calculator includes sand because bedding and joint quantities are common planning questions, not because sand is a structural gravel.

On these figures, crushed stone is the least expensive material per ton and river rock is roughly 3.4 times its median price. That is a price observation, not a recommendation: a river rock border can still be the right design choice where no vehicle drives on it.

How to choose without overthinking it

Will vehicles drive or park on it?

Choose an angular, graded crushed stone and build a proper base. Do not use pea gravel or river rock as the running surface.

Is it primarily for bare feet or play?

Pea gravel is the more comfortable choice. Use a rigid edge and keep it away from vehicle traffic.

Is the look the main requirement?

River rock offers size and colour, but price and weight rise quickly. Use it as an accent rather than the whole driveway.

What does your local yard actually stock?

Haul distance can matter more than the national median. Ask for the gradation, loose density, price unit and delivery fee in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Which gravel is best for a driveway?

Crushed stone is the usual choice because its angular faces interlock when compacted. The surface also needs a properly prepared and compacted base, not just a layer of stone. Pea gravel and river rock are rounded and shift under vehicle traffic.

Is pea gravel cheaper than crushed stone?

In the October 2026 U.S. published-price data used here, the median was $47 per ton for pea gravel and $31 per ton for crushed stone. Local availability and haul distance can reverse that relationship, so compare quotes from nearby yards.

How much area does one ton of gravel cover?

Coverage depends on density and depth. One ton of crushed stone covers about 120 square feet at 2 inches, 80 square feet at 3 inches or 60 square feet at 4 inches. One ton of pea gravel covers about 114, 76 or 57 square feet at the same depths. These figures exclude the waste allowance.

Can river rock be used on a driveway?

River rock is not recommended for a driveway. The stones are rounded and do not interlock under load, so they roll and shift under tyres. It is better suited to decorative beds, dry creek beds, borders and erosion-control features where vehicles do not drive.

How much extra gravel should I order?

Order about 10% more than the geometric estimate for compaction, spillage and uneven ground. Increase the allowance to about 15% for loose rounded stone, sloped sites or a soft subgrade. Confirm the supplier's unit, because some yards quote by short ton and others by cubic yard.

What is the difference between crushed stone and gravel?

Gravel is a broad term for loose rock, while crushed stone is mechanically broken rock with angular faces. Pea gravel is naturally rounded. Names alone do not tell you the gradation, hardness or drainage performance, so ask for the specification and a test density.
