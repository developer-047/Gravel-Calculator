How much pea gravel do I need?

For a rectangular area, multiply length × width × depth in feet, then divide by 27 to get cubic yards. A 10 ft × 10 ft area at 3 in deep needs about 0.93 cubic yards, 1.31 short tons or 53 × 50 lb bags of pea gravel at the calculator's 105 lb/ft³ density assumption.

Default example (10 ft × 10 ft at 3 in):

  • Area: 10 ft × 10 ft = 100 ft²
  • Depth: 3 in ÷ 12 = 0.25 ft
  • Volume: 100 ft² × 0.25 ft = 25 ft³
  • Cubic yards: 25 ft³ ÷ 27 = 0.93 yd³
  • Weight: 25 ft³ × 105 lb/ft³ = 2,625 lb = 1.31 short tons
  • Bags: 2,625 lb ÷ 50 lb = 52.5, rounded up to 53 bags

The calculator rounds bag counts up so you do not run short. Enter your own dimensions, depth and optional waste or compaction allowance above for a project-specific estimate.

How to calculate pea gravel for a path or patio

Measure the area in feet, convert the depth from inches to feet, multiply to find cubic feet, then divide by 27. For a 20 ft × 4 ft path at 2 in deep: 20 × 4 = 80 ft²; 2 in ÷ 12 = 0.1667 ft; 80 × 0.1667 = 13.33 ft³; 13.33 ÷ 27 = 0.49 yd³. At 105 lb/ft³ that is 1,400 lb, or 0.70 short tons and 28 × 50 lb bags.

Add a waste allowance for spillage, uneven ground and settlement. A 10% waste allowance turns 0.49 yd³ into 0.54 yd³ (about 1,512 lb, 0.76 short tons and 31 bags). Compaction is applied after waste, not added to it: the calculator multiplies by (1 + waste%) and then by (1 + compaction%).

For circular areas, use π × radius² × depth. For an L-shaped area, split it into two rectangles, calculate each, and add the volumes. The calculator above handles all three shapes.

How deep should pea gravel be?

Most decorative and pedestrian uses need 2–3 in of pea gravel. Playgrounds are different: the CPSC guidance is based on a compressed depth of 9 in for a fall height up to 5 ft, with an initial fill of about 12 in to allow for compression.

Recommended pea gravel depth by project type
Project Typical depth Notes
Garden path or walkway 2–3 in Use edging to stop the stones spreading.
Patio surface 2–3 in Compact the base first; pea gravel is a loose surface.
Decorative border 2 in A thinner skim is enough for visual cover.
Playground 9 in compressed Start near 12 in; CPSC lists protection to a 5 ft fall height.
Driveway surface Not recommended alone Rounded stones shift under vehicle loads; use angular stone.

Depth ranges are planning guidance for typical residential projects. Local site conditions, edging and drainage can change the right depth.

Pea gravel for playgrounds: CPSC safety depths

The CPSC Public Playground Safety Handbook lists 9 in of compressed pea gravel as protecting to a fall height of 5 ft, not 7 ft. Because loose fill compresses at least 25% over time, the initial fill should be about 12 in so the maintained depth stays at 9 in.

  • Maintain at least 9 in of compressed pea gravel under and around the equipment.
  • Check depth regularly, especially under swings and at slide exits where material displaces.
  • Use containment edging and plan for drainage so the material does not wash away.
  • Do not install loose fill over concrete or asphalt.
  • Pea gravel is not impact-tested in the same way as engineered wood fiber or rubber surfacing. Check the equipment manufacturer's requirements and local rules.

The calculator's playground preset uses depth as an input, but it cannot certify a playground. If the fall height is above 5 ft, use a surfacing material with a documented critical-height rating for that height.

Source: U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, Public Playground Safety Handbook (Publication 325), Table 2. Accessed November 9, 2026. cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/325.pdf

Pea gravel vs crushed stone

Pea gravel is rounded and smooth; crushed stone is angular. That shape difference matters more than color: angular stone interlocks and drains well under traffic, while pea gravel shifts and is better for loose decorative surfaces and playground fill.

How many bags of pea gravel are in a cubic yard?

At 105 lb/ft³, one cubic yard of pea gravel weighs about 2,835 lb. Divide that by the bag weight: about 57 × 50 lb bags, 71 × 40 lb bags or 48 × 60 lb bags per cubic yard. Bag counts are rounded up.

The "bags per short ton" column depends only on bag size, not material: 2,000 lb ÷ 50 lb = 40 bags, 2,000 ÷ 40 = 50, and 2,000 ÷ 60 = 33.3, rounded up to 34. The bag calculator handles pallet counts and bulk-vs-bag comparisons.

What does pea gravel cost?

This calculator does not publish a default price because delivered gravel prices vary by region, quantity, supplier and season. Enter the price per short ton your supplier quoted, add any delivery fee and tax rate, and the calculator shows an estimated total. If you leave the price blank, the cost card stays hidden.

For a fair comparison, ask suppliers for a delivered price that includes:

  • Material cost per ton or cubic yard.
  • Delivery fee and minimum order size.
  • Tax and any fuel or handling surcharge.
  • Whether the price is based on short tons (2,000 lb) or metric tonnes (1,000 kg).

Bagged pea gravel usually costs more per ton than bulk delivery, but bags can be practical for small areas or sites with limited truck access. Compare the total delivered cost, not just the shelf price.

Common pea gravel mistakes

  • Using cubic feet as cubic yards. Divide cubic feet by 27 before ordering. A 100 ft² area at 3 in is 25 ft³, or 0.93 yd³.
  • Skipping waste. Uneven ground, edging gaps and spillage are normal. A 10% allowance is a reasonable starting point.
  • Assuming pea gravel is a playground safety certificate. CPSC lists 9 in compressed for a 5 ft fall height; deeper or higher equipment needs a documented impact-tested surface.
  • Using pea gravel as a driveway base. Rounded stones do not interlock under vehicle loads. Use crusher run or #304 for the base and angular stone for the surface.
  • Forgetting containment. Without edging, pea gravel spreads into lawns and drains.

Frequently asked questions

How much pea gravel do I need?
Multiply length × width × depth in feet, then divide by 27. A 10 ft × 10 ft area at 3 in deep needs about 0.93 yd³, 1.31 short tons or 53 × 50 lb bags.
How deep should pea gravel be for a playground?

What is pea gravel best used for?

Does pea gravel compact?
