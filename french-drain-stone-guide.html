French Drain Stone Guide

A detailed guide to calculating stone for French drains, with focus on pipe deduction and unique angles.

Last updated: November 9, 2026

Why Pipe Deduction Matters

When calculating gravel for a French drain, most contractors forget to subtract the volume of the pipe running through the trench. This oversight leads to over-ordering by 5–10% and unnecessary costs. Our guide explains how to calculate pipe volume and integrate it into your calculations.

Step-by-Step Pipe Volume Calculation

The volume of a pipe is calculated using the formula for a cylinder: Volume = π × (diameter/2)² × length. This volume must be subtracted from the total trench volume before calculating gravel needs.

Pipe Diameter (inches) Pipe Length (feet) Pipe Volume (ft³) Pipe Volume (yd³) Gravel Saved (yd³)

6 in 50 ft 10.42 ft³ 0.38 yd³ 0.38 yd³
8 in 50 ft 18.85 ft³ 0.69 yd³ 0.69 yd³

Example: A 4″ pipe in a 50-ft trench (as in the worked example) saves 0.16 yd³ of gravel, which is equivalent to 4.36 ft³ or approximately 5 bags of 50 lb gravel.

How to Calculate Pipe Volume

Follow these steps to calculate the volume of any pipe running through your French drain trench:

  1. Measure the pipe diameter in inches. Standard sizes are 4″, 6″, and 8″ for residential French drains.
  2. Measure the pipe length in feet. This is the length of pipe running through the trench, not the total trench length.
  3. Calculate the radius. Divide the diameter by 2 and convert to feet (divide inches by 12).
  4. Calculate the volume in cubic feet. Use the formula: Volume = π × radius² × length
  5. Convert to cubic yards. Divide the cubic feet by 27 (since 1 yd³ = 27 ft³).
  6. Subtract from trench volume. Calculate the total trench volume (length × width × depth) and subtract the pipe volume.

Pipe Volume Calculator Example

Let's calculate the pipe volume for a 6″ diameter pipe running 50 ft through a French drain trench:

  • Pipe diameter: 6 inches
  • Pipe length: 50 feet
  • Radius: 6 in ÷ 2 = 3 in = 0.5 ft
  • Volume: π × (0.5 ft)² × 50 ft = 3.1416 × 0.25 × 50 = 39.27 ft³
  • Convert to cubic yards: 39.27 ft³ ÷ 27 = 1.45 yd³
  • This means you save 1.45 yd³ of gravel by including the pipe in your calculation.

Tips for Contractors and DIYers

  • Always measure the actual pipe length running through the trench, not the total trench length.
  • Use the same pipe diameter as specified by the manufacturer or as installed.
  • Include pipe volume in all French drain calculations, even if the client says "no pipe."
  • For large projects, consider creating a spreadsheet to automate pipe volume calculations.
  • Verify pipe dimensions with the supplier or contractor before ordering gravel.
  • Document pipe dimensions in your project notes for future reference and accuracy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to calculate pipe volume for all French drain projects?
Yes, even if no pipe is specified. The calculator defaults to 0 pipe volume, but you should enter the actual pipe dimensions if a pipe is planned.
What if I use a different pipe material?
The calculation is based on dimensions, not material. The volume formula works the same for PVC, clay, or concrete pipes.
How much does pipe diameter affect the calculation?
Pipe diameter has a significant impact. A 4″ pipe saves 0.16 yd³ for a 50-ft trench, while an 8″ pipe saves 0.69 yd³ – a difference of 0.53 yd³ (about 16 bags of 50 lb gravel).
Can I use the calculator without pipe deduction?
Yes, but you should manually subtract the pipe volume if a pipe is planned. The calculator includes a pipe diameter field for this purpose.
Is pipe deduction required by CPSC?
No, but it's a best practice for proper drainage and cost estimation. CPSC focuses on maintained depth, not pipe calculations.
