How to use this calculator

Enter the trench length, width and depth, then the pipe diameter if one runs through it; the calculator deducts the pipe volume and returns cubic yards, short tons and bag counts.

  1. Step 1: Enter trench dimensions.

    Input the length and width of your French drain trench. Use feet and inches for residential projects, or metres and centimetres for metric. Typical residential French drains are 12″ wide and 18–24″ deep.

  2. Step 2: Enter pipe diameter (if applicable).

    If a perforated pipe will run through the trench, enter its diameter. The calculator automatically subtracts the pipe volume from the total gravel needed. This is the key differentiator from generic gravel calculators.

  3. Step 3: Select stone type and set allowances.

    Choose #57 stone (the most common for French drains), drainage rock, or pea gravel. Set waste allowance (typically 10% for clean stone) and compaction allowance (5% is standard).

  4. Step 4: Review results.

    The calculator shows cubic yards/m³, short tons/metric tonnes, bag counts and pallet quantities. Filter fabric is estimated from trench width, depth and overlap, then converted to linear feet at a 3 ft roll width.

  5. Step 5: Order extra and verify slope.

    Order about 10% extra for compaction and settling. A minimum 1% slope (1/8″ per foot of trench length) is required for proper drainage. Verify with a local contractor for structural work.

Worked example: 50‑ft French drain

A 50 ft × 12″ × 18″ trench with a 4″ pipe needs 3.05 cubic yards of #57 stone — 4.11 short tons or 165 × 50 lb bags — after 10% waste, 5% compaction and a 0.16 yd³ pipe deduction.

Inputs:

  • Trench length: 50 ft
  • Trench width: 12 in
  • Trench depth: 18 in
  • Pipe diameter: 4 in
  • Pipe length: 50 ft
  • Stone type: #57 (crushed stone at 100 lb/ft³)
  • Waste: 10%
  • Compaction: 5%
  • Bag size: 50 lb

Results (#57 stone at 100 lb/ft³):

  • Trench volume: 50 ft × 1 ft × 1.5 ft = 75 ft³ = 2.78 yd³
  • With 10% waste and 5% compaction: 75 × 1.10 × 1.05 = 86.63 ft³ = 3.21 yd³
  • Pipe volume: π × (4 in ÷ 12 ÷ 2)² × 50 ft = 4.36 ft³ = 0.16 yd³
  • Net stone: 86.63 − 4.36 = 82.26 ft³ = 3.05 yd³
  • Weight: 82.26 ft³ × 100 lb/ft³ = 8,226 lb ≈ 4.11 short tons (3.73 metric tonnes)
  • Bags: 8,226 lb ÷ 50 lb = 164.5, rounded up to 165 bags
  • Pallets: 5 pallets (4 full + 5 loose bags at 40 bags per pallet)

The pipe deduction is 0.16 yd³, about 5% of the adjusted trench volume—not 10%.

Without the pipe deduction the same trench needs 3.21 yd³ (about 4.33 short tons and 174 bags), so deducting the pipe saves about 0.16 yd³, 0.22 short tons and 9 bags.

French drain depth and slope

Slope a French drain at a minimum of 1% (1/8″ per foot), and keep the trench 12–24″ deep for residential drains.

  • Minimum slope: 1% (1/8″ per foot of trench length, or 1 cm per meter). Fall less than this and water may pool.
  • Typical depth: 12–24 inches for residential drains; deeper for high-water-table sites.
  • Outlet: The drain must day-light at the lower end or connect to a sump/catch basin.
  • Warning: This calculator provides estimates only. Confirm slope and design with a licensed contractor or engineer before excavation.

What size gravel for a French drain?

#57 stone (¾–1″) is the standard French drain gravel because it drains well and resists clogging; #4 (1½″) suits heavy-duty flow, and pea gravel is not recommended as the primary fill.

#57 Stone (¾″)

Most common for French drains. Provides good drainage while resisting clogging. The ¾″ size allows water to flow while keeping fines out.

#4 Gravel (1½″)

Larger stone; used for heavy-duty drainage or when extra void space is needed. More material per cubic yard, higher cost.

Pea Gravel (½″ rounded)

Smooth, rounded particles pack more tightly and can reduce drainage speed. Not recommended for primary French drains unless combined with filter fabric.

Why #57 wins: angular faces interlock when compacted, creating stable yet permeable fill. Rounded pea gravel can shift and reduce void space over time.

Filter fabric: how much do you need?

A 50 ft trench, 12″ wide and 18″ deep, needs about 300 ft² of filter fabric — roughly 100 linear ft from a 3 ft roll.

Calculation: fabric width = trench width + (2 × trench depth) + (2 × overlap), then area = trench length × fabric width.

For a 50 ft long, 12″ wide, 18″ deep trench with 12″ of overlap on each side: width = 1 ft + 3 ft + 2 ft = 6 ft; area = 50 ft × 6 ft = 300 ft². At a 3 ft roll width that is about 100 linear ft of fabric.

Use 12–18 inches of overlap at seams and at the trench edges. If the pipe or drain field is wider than the trench, size the fabric to clear it.

Do not skip filter fabric: without it, native soil can fill the gravel voids and reduce drainage performance, especially in fine‑textured soils.

Common mistakes

These five avoidable errors are common causes of French drain over-orders: no pipe deduction, the wrong stone size, no slope, no filter fabric, or no waste allowance.

  • No pipe deduction: Forgetting to subtract the pipe volume leads to over-ordering. For a 4″ pipe in a 50-ft trench, the error is about 0.16 yd³ (5%).
  • Wrong stone size: Using pea gravel without adequate fabric, or #4 stone when #57 suffices, affects both cost and drainage speed.
  • Skipping slope: A French drain without minimum 1% slope will pool water instead of draining it.
  • No filter fabric: Soil migration into the gravel destroys permeability over time.
  • Under‑ordering: Not adding waste (10%) and compaction (5%) allowances means you'll run short mid‑project.

FAQ

How deep should a French drain be?
12–24 inches for residential; deeper if the water table is high or the driveway/area requires it. Minimum 1% slope required.
Do I need a pipe in my French drain?
Not required, but a perforated pipe collects and directs water more effectively, especially in high‑water‑table areas.
How much gravel per foot of French drain?
About 0.06 cubic yards per foot for a 12″ wide, 18″ deep trench with #57 stone before allowances (1 ft × 1.5 ft = 1.5 ft³ = 0.056 yd³). Add waste and compaction, and deduct any pipe volume.
Can I use pea gravel instead of #57?
Possible, but #57 is recommended. If using pea gravel, increase filter fabric coverage and order 10–15% extra.
How much does a French drain cost?
It depends on the stone, pipe, fabric, excavation and local labour rates. This page estimates gravel, pipe and fabric quantities only. Enter your supplier's price per ton above for a material cost, and get contractor quotes for installation.
Is 10% waste allowance enough?
For #57 stone in a typical residential French drain, 10% waste + 5% compaction is standard. Add 15% if the subgrade is soft or the stone is rounded (pea gravel).
