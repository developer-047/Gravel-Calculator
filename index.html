How to measure a driveway, patio or L-shaped area

Calculating gravel for a rectangle is straightforward. Real projects are often less tidy: an L-shaped driveway, a curved patio or a long gravel road with a crown. The reliable method is to divide the area into simple rectangles, measure each one, calculate the volume for each and add them together. The calculator's L-shape mode does the addition for you.

Measure the area, not the pile

Work in the same unit for every measurement. For a rectangle, measure the finished length and width. For a circle, measure the diameter across the centre, not the radius. For an L-shape, split the plan into Section A and Section B and measure both. If the edges are irregular, measure the widest and narrowest points and use an average — then add a little extra.

Why depth is the number that usually goes wrong

Most DIY mistakes come from depth, not area. Two inches of loose gravel can disappear into a soft subgrade or compact to less than the finished thickness you expected. Six inches on a garden path is usually money wasted. Use these starting points:

  • Walking paths: 2 to 3 inches (50–75 mm) of compacted material.
  • Residential driveways: 4 to 6 inches (100–150 mm), usually built in two or three compacted lifts.
  • Decorative borders: 1 to 2 inches (25–50 mm), enough to cover the surface.
  • Heavy vehicles or poor subgrade: a thicker engineered base may be needed; confirm with a local contractor.

Add about 10% for compaction and waste

With both allowances at 0%, the calculator returns the geometric volume. Set the waste slider to about 10% to cover spillage, uneven ground and the material that stays in the truck. Loose rounded stone, sloped sites and soft subgrades may need 15%. Add the compaction preset for a driveway or walkway as well; the two allowances are applied independently. It is usually cheaper to have a small surplus than to pay a second delivery charge for a few bags.

Pea gravel, crushed stone or river rock?

The material changes the weight, the stability and the price. Pea gravel is smooth and comfortable underfoot, but it shifts under tyres. Crushed stone has angular faces that lock together, which is why it is the usual choice for driveways and base layers. River rock is decorative and heavy, and it is a poor surface for vehicle traffic. Check the density on your supplier's price sheet; local rock can differ from the typical values used here.

Frequently asked questions

Practical answers about depth, waste, density and choosing a material.

How deep should a gravel driveway be?

For a residential driveway, plan on 4 to 6 inches (100–150 mm) of compacted gravel over a prepared base. Build it in 2 to 3 inch lifts and compact each lift before adding the next. Paths usually need 2 to 3 inches.

Site note

Heavy vehicles, poor drainage or a soft subgrade may need a thicker engineered base. Confirm the build-up with a local contractor rather than simply adding more surface gravel.

How much extra gravel should I order for compaction and waste?

Order about 10% more than the calculator's geometric estimate. Increase that to about 15% for loose rounded stone, sloped ground or a soft subgrade, where more material is lost to compaction and uneven spreading.

Example

A 10 cubic yard estimate becomes an order of about 11 cubic yards. A second delivery for a small shortfall usually costs more than the surplus.

How do I calculate gravel for an L-shaped or irregular area?

Divide the plan into simple rectangles, calculate each one separately, then add the volumes. The calculator's L-shape mode does the addition for you.

  1. Split the area into Section A and Section B.
  2. Measure the length and width of each section in the same unit.
  3. Enter both sections in L-shape mode. For a curved edge, use an average width and add a slightly larger waste allowance.
How much does a cubic yard of gravel weigh?

Weight depends on the material and on whether it is loose or compacted. The calculator uses these typical loose bulk densities:

Pea gravel 105 lb/ft³ · ≈ 2,835 lb/yd³
Crushed stone 100 lb/ft³ · ≈ 2,700 lb/yd³
Sand 110 lb/ft³ · ≈ 2,970 lb/yd³
River rock 108 lb/ft³ · ≈ 2,916 lb/yd³

Note: Wet material and compacted stone can weigh more. If your supplier publishes a test density, use that instead. See the full density table and sources.

What is the difference between pea gravel and crushed stone?

Pea gravel

Small, rounded and smooth. It drains well and is comfortable underfoot, but it shifts under tyres. Best for paths, borders and decorative beds rather than a driveway surface.

Crushed stone

Angular and rough. The faces interlock when compacted, which is why it is the usual choice for driveways and structural base layers.

How the estimate is calculated

The calculator uses the standard volume formula and a material-specific loose bulk density. Waste and compaction are explicit, user-controlled allowances rather than hidden additions: leave both sliders at 0% to see the raw geometric quantity, or add an allowance to plan the order.

Step 1

Find the area

Rectangle: length × width. Circle: π × radius². L-shape: Section A + Section B.

Step 2

Multiply by depth

Convert depth to feet, then calculate area × depth. Divide by 27 for cubic yards.

Step 3

Apply density

Convert volume to weight using the selected material's density, then to short tons or metric tonnes and 50 lb or 25 kg bags.

cubic yards = area (ft²) × depth (ft) ÷ 27
cubic metres = cubic yards × 0.764554857984
short tons = cubic yards × 27 × density (lb/ft³) ÷ 2,000
metric tonnes = kilograms ÷ 1,000
50 lb bags = total pounds ÷ 50 · 25 kg bags = total kilograms ÷ 25
adjusted volume = base volume × (1 + waste ÷ 100) × (1 + compaction ÷ 100)

Results are rounded for readability. Bags are rounded up. With both allowances at 0%, the result is the geometric quantity; add about 10% waste for typical projects, or up to 15% for loose, sloped or soft sites.
