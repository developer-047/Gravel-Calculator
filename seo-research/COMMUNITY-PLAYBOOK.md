# Phase 4 — Task 4-5: community presence playbook

**Status 2026-11-10:** playbook + 7 drafted answers. **Nothing posted.** Every action in this file
requires the owner's own account: Reddit and Quora both treat a brand-new account that only answers
questions in one topic as the spam pattern they are trying to remove.

**Evidence split:** the Reddit policy section is sourced (links below). The drafts are ours and untested
in the wild — no claim is made that any of them will be accepted by a moderator.

## 1. What Reddit's rules actually say (checked 2026-11-10)

Three things are commonly repeated and only one is current:

1. **The old self-promotion wiki** (`reddit.com/wiki/selfpromotion`) states a 10% rule of thumb — and the
   page itself says "This page is no longer updated" and points to Reddit Help.
   `[SERP-observed]`
2. **The current Help Center spam policy** does not publish a numeric ratio. Its test is behavioural:
   whether an account's contributions consist primarily of links to a business the user runs or
   benefits from. `[SERP-observed]` (quoted in several 2026 policy audits that fetched the Help Center).
3. **Subreddit rules govern.** A community may ban links outright, allow promotion only in a weekly
   thread, require moderator approval, or set its own ratio. Independent 2026 audits of subreddit
   rulebooks found link restrictions in roughly half of the communities they sampled. `[SERP-observed]`

**Consequence for us:** do not cite "the 10% rule" as policy in any plan or email. Read the specific
subreddit's rules before contributing, and assume links are the trigger, not mentions.

**Quora:** its self-promotion policy was not verified for this document. Check Quora's current policy on
self-answered questions and links before posting. `[Needs owner input]`

## 2. Operating rules (non-negotiable)

1. The owner's account, the owner's name or a stated pseudonym, disclosed affiliation in the profile
   ("I run a gravel calculator"). No fake personas, no purchased accounts.
2. Answer first, link second, and only when the link adds something the comment does not already say.
3. Never post the same URL in more than one thread, and never in more than one subreddit.
4. No vote manipulation of any kind, including asking anyone to upvote.
5. If a comment is removed, do not repost it. Ask the moderators once.
6. Log every answer: date, subreddit/space, question, whether a link was included, outcome.

## 4. Seven drafted answers

Rules for using these: rewrite in your own voice, answer the question that was actually asked, and cut
the link if the comment reads as promotion without it. Each draft cites only figures that exist on our
pages.

**Draft 1 — "How much gravel for a 10x10 patio at 2 inches?"**
> 10 × 10 = 100 sq ft. At 2 in deep that is 100 × (2/12) = 16.7 ft³, and 16.7 ÷ 27 = 0.62 yd³.
> At about 1.42 tons per yard for pea gravel that is roughly 0.9 tons, so about 35 × 50 lb bags if you
> are bagging it. If the patio is going over soil rather than a compacted base, add 10% for settling.
> The depth is what people get wrong, not the maths — 2 in is right for a patio you will sit on, 4 in
> if anything heavier than furniture goes on it.

**Draft 2 — "Why did my French drain use so much more stone than I calculated?"**
> Two usual causes. First, the trench is usually wider than people assume once it is dug — a 12 in
> bucket does not leave a 12 in trench. Second, compaction: loose stone settles 5-15%, so the order has
> to cover the settled depth. The pipe itself is the one thing that works in your favour: a 6 in pipe
> through 50 ft displaces about 0.36 yd³, roughly 20 bags, which most guides never subtract.

**Draft 3 — "Is a cubic yard the same as a ton of gravel?"**
> No, and the gap is about a third. A yard of typical crushed stone is roughly 1.35 tons; pea gravel is
> closer to 1.42 because it packs differently. Suppliers sell by whichever unit their scale uses, so ask
> which one the quote is in before comparing two quotes — this is where people think one supplier is
> cheaper when they are quoting different units.

**Draft 4 — "How deep should a gravel driveway be?"**
> Two layers, not one: 4-6 in of compacted base (crusher run or #57) and 2-3 in of surface gravel. A
> single 2 in layer over dirt will disappear into the mud in one wet season. If you are topping up an
> existing drive, 1-2 in of surface stone is usually enough.

**Draft 5 — "How many bags of gravel equal a yard?"**
> At 50 lb bags and about 2,835 lb per yard of pea gravel, 57 bags. For crushed stone at 1.35 tons per
> yard it is about 54. Most people round to "about 50" and then come up short, so buy the extra bag —
> and above roughly half a yard, bulk delivery is usually cheaper than bags once you count the trips.

**Draft 6 — "What size stone for a French drain?"**
> 3/4 in clean crushed stone is the standard for the surround, with 1.5-3 in stone used in some
> specifications for the trench body. "Clean" matters more than the exact size: fines clog the fabric and
> the drain stops working. Washed #57 is the usual answer in the US; avoid crusher run in the trench.

**Draft 7 — "Pea gravel or crushed stone for a path?"**
> Pea gravel is comfortable underfoot but rounds and migrates — it needs edging and it is harder to
> compact. Crushed stone locks together and stays put, at the cost of being rougher on bare feet. If the
> path is steep or gets wheeled traffic, crushed stone. If it is a garden path you walk on in socks,
> pea gravel over a proper base.
