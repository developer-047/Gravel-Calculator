## 1. Qualification rules (apply to every future row)

A target counts as qualified only if all four are true:

1. **Topical.** The page is about gravel, drainage stone, aggregate quantity, or driveway/path material.
2. **Editorial.** It is an article, guide, resource or calculator page — not a product listing, a forum
   thread, or our own page.
3. **Not a direct competitor in intent.** Pages whose own job is "gravel calculator / how much gravel"
   are competitors for the same query. They rarely link out and would compete for the link. Log them
   in §3 instead.
4. **Link-plausible.** The page already links out to suppliers, references, tools or standards, so an
   editorial link is not out of character. Brand-controlled manufacturer sites and large magazines are
   low probability, not zero — they are kept, ranked low, and worked last.

Assets to offer, in order: French drain stone guide → density chart → widget embed → price index
(price index only after its provenance item is resolved).

## 4. How to work the list

The plan's weekly cadence is 20 personalised emails with one follow-up after 7 days. At 69 domains that
is three and a half weeks of sending, then the follow-up pass.

Send order:

1. **Rows 1-20** (the high-likelihood block). These pages already do the calculation and the pitch is
   short: "you explain the cubic yards, here is the tons step you are missing."
2. **Rows 21-55**. Reference pages; lead with the density chart as a data citation, not as a tool.
3. **Rows 56-80**. Big publishers and lower-priority supplier guides. Expect silence; send once and move on.
4. **Rows 81-100**. Own-tool suppliers (Eastern Landscape, NDS). Lowest priority: they have a calculator
   and a link would compete with it.

Per email:

- One paragraph on what their page says and what is missing. Nothing generic.
- One link to the specific asset (density chart, French drain guide, or widget) deep-linked to the table
  that helps them, e.g. `#density-table`.
- Offer the citation text we already publish, since that is lower friction than a link request.
- No follow-up more than once. No "just bumping this".

Anchor mix: keep exact-match ("gravel calculator") under 30% of links earned. Preferred: "gravel density
chart", "Pro Gravel Calculator", or a plain URL. Natural mentions outperform exact-match on a site this
young, and the plan forbids anchor stuffing.
