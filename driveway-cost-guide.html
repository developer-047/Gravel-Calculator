Cost guide

How much does a gravel driveway cost in 2026?

Published U.S. ranges put a gravel driveway at about $1.25 to $4.00 per square foot installed. A national average of roughly $1,800 is a useful anchor, but the spread is wide because grading, delivery, drainage and access often cost more than the stone.

By Pro Gravel Calculator · Reviewed November 1, 2026 · Method & sources

The short answer

  • Most projects: about $500 to $3,500, with a national average near $1,800 (Angi, 2026).
  • Per square foot: about $1.25 to $4.00 installed (HomeAdvisor planning range); smaller jobs usually sit higher per square foot.
  • A 400–600 sq ft double-car driveway: about $800 to $2,200 installed (Angi, 2026).
  • The stone itself: often a minority of the total. Crushed stone had a median published price of $31 per ton in October 2026, before delivery.

These are published national planning ranges, not a quotation for your property. Local labor, haul distance and site conditions can move the number substantially.

Material costs per ton and per cubic yard

Suppliers may quote by the short ton or by the cubic yard. The conversion depends on the material's bulk density, so a cheap price per ton can still be an expensive price per cubic yard. This table uses the calculator's loose densities and the October 2026 published medians.

For a driveway surface, crushed stone is the usual starting point because angular faces interlock under compaction. Pea gravel and river rock are rounded and shift under tyres. The full material comparison explains where each one works.

Worked example: 600 sq ft at 4 inches

A 600 sq ft area at 4 inches deep is 200 cubic feet, or 7.41 cubic yards. With a 10% allowance for compaction and waste, the order quantity is about 8.15 cubic yards. At the calculator's crushed-stone density, that is roughly 11.0 short tons.

What drives the final price

Area and depth

Volume rises with both. Doubling the depth roughly doubles the stone, the deliveries and the compaction time.

Base preparation

Excavation, grading and a compacted base course are separate from the surface stone. Soft or wet ground may need geotextile and thicker layers.

Material and haul distance

Decorative stone can cost several times more than crusher run. Distance from the quarry adds trucking and may trigger a minimum load charge.

Access and slope

A truck that cannot reach the site means bulk bags, a smaller vehicle or more hand work. Slopes need shaping and drainage to prevent washout.

Drainage

Grading away from structures, a crown, culverts or a French drain can add cost, but skipping them usually costs more later.

Edging and containment

Without an edge, stone migrates into beds and lawns. Edging is usually priced by the linear foot, not the square foot.

How to estimate the stone for your driveway

  1. Measure the finished area. Length × width for a rectangle; split an L-shape into two rectangles; use the diameter for a circle.
  2. Choose the depth. Use 4–6 inches of compacted surface gravel for a residential driveway, or 2–3 inches for a path. Calculate the base layer separately.
  3. Convert to cubic yards. Area (ft²) × depth (ft) ÷ 27. A 600 sq ft area at 4 inches is 7.41 yd³.
  4. Apply the density. The calculator converts cubic yards to short tons using the selected material's loose density.
  5. Add the allowance. About 10% extra, or up to 15% for loose, sloped or soft sites.
  6. Price it with a local quote. Multiply the order weight by the supplier's per-ton price, then add delivery and site work.

How to compare gravel driveway quotes

Ask for a written, itemized scope rather than a single lump sum. The following lines make quotes comparable and expose the assumptions that change the price.

  • ☐Area, depth and calculated volume, with the waste allowance shown separately.
  • ☐Material name, gradation or specification, and the density used to convert volume to weight.
  • ☐Tons or cubic yards supplied, price per unit, and whether delivery is included.
  • ☐Base preparation: excavation depth, grading, geotextile, and compaction in lifts.
  • ☐Drainage work, edging, culverts and disposal of spoil or old surface material.
  • ☐Tax, permit requirements, start date and payment schedule.

Be cautious of a quote that cannot state the tonnage, the base depth or the compaction method. Those are not administrative details; they are the difference between a driveway and a pile of stone. Verify licensing and insurance where your state or locality requires it.

Ways to reduce cost without skipping the essentials

  • Keep the layout simple. Curves, tight access and long runs add labor and equipment time. Reducing the paved area is the most reliable saving.
  • Buy local. Compare two or three nearby yards. Haul distance is part of the price even when delivery looks "free".
  • Use the right stone in the right layer. Crusher run or a graded base stone is usually less expensive than a decorative surface stone. Do not downgrade the surface where it carries traffic.
  • Do the spreading yourself only if you can compact properly. A plate compactor and enough time are part of the job, not optional extras.
  • Phase the work. A firm, well-drained base first and a surface course later can spread the cost, provided the first phase drains correctly.
  • Do not skip drainage or edging to hit a budget. Both are cheaper now than repairing ruts, washout and migrating stone later.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average cost of a gravel driveway in 2026?

Angi's 2026 gravel driveway guide reports a national average of about $1,800, with most homeowners paying between $500 and $3,500. HomeAdvisor's published planning range is $1.25 to $4.00 per square foot installed. These are national ranges, not a quote for a specific property.

How much does a 600 square foot gravel driveway cost?

Angi's 2026 size table puts a 400 to 600 square foot double-car driveway at $800 to $2,200 installed. For the material alone, 600 square feet at 4 inches is about 7.41 cubic yards; with a 10% allowance that is 8.15 cubic yards, or about 11 tons of crushed stone. At the October 2026 median that is roughly $341, with a middle-half range of $220 to $528 before delivery.

How much does gravel cost per ton in the U.S.?

GoodGravel's October 2026 published-price data gives a median of $31 per ton for crushed stone ($20 to $48 middle half), $47 for pea gravel ($34 to $66), $106 for river rock ($65 to $177) and $48 for masonry sand ($34 to $60). These are material-only prices before delivery.

How much does gravel delivery cost?

Delivery is often included in a delivered per-ton price, but when it is billed separately HomeAdvisor's planning guidance is about $5 per mile. Many yards charge by the load or apply a minimum order, so ask for the delivery line separately and confirm the truck size and access requirements.

Is it cheaper to build a gravel driveway yourself?

Doing the spreading and compaction yourself can remove the labor line, but it does not remove the material, delivery, base preparation, equipment rental or disposal costs. It also depends on having the time, tools and physical capacity for the work. Compare a DIY material quote with a fully installed quote that lists the same scope.

How deep should a gravel driveway be?

A common residential build-up is 4 to 6 inches of compacted gravel over a prepared base, installed in 2 to 3 inch lifts. Paths usually need 2 to 3 inches. Heavy vehicles, poor drainage or a soft subgrade may need a thicker engineered base; confirm the specification with a local contractor or supplier.
