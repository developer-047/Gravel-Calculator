Driveway Gravel Calculator Overview

Calculate the right amount of gravel for your driveway project, including base and surface layers.

Gravel is typically sold by the cubic yard or by the ton. The calculator converts your dimensions into the quantity you need, accounting for material density and compaction.

  • Select your material from presets or enter a custom density (lb/ft³).
  • Choose your layer configuration: base only, surface only, or base + surface.
  • Enter your driveway dimensions (length × width, or diameter for circular driveways).
  • Select depth in inches (base typically 4″, surface typically 2″).
  • The calculator shows cubic yards, tons, bag counts, and pallet breakdown.

Material Selection

Choose from common driveway gravel types with preset densities.

Pea Gravel (105 lb/ft³):

  • Decorative, smooth rounded stones
  • Typically used for paths and decorative areas
  • Less compaction, more shifting under vehicle weight

#57 Stone (100 lb/ft³):

  • ¾″–1″ crushed stone, good drainage
  • Common for driveway surface
  • Interlocks when compacted, stable under traffic

Crusher Run / 304 Stone (105 lb/ft³):

  • Crushed stone with fines, compacts densely
  • Ideal for base/subgrade layer
  • Provides stable foundation and frost protection

Custom density:

  • Enter your material's lb/ft³ if different from presets
  • Common ranges: 95–130 lb/ft³ depending on rock type

Layer Configuration

A typical residential driveway has two layers.

Base layer (recommended 4″):

  • Material: Crusher Run or 304 Stone
  • Purpose: Load distribution, frost protection, stable foundation
  • Typical depth: 4″ (range 3-6″ depending on soil conditions)

Surface layer (recommended 2″):

  • Material: #57 Stone or Pea Gravel
  • Purpose: Driving surface, drainage, aesthetics
  • Typical depth: 2″ (range 1.5-3″)

Total typical thickness:

  • 6″ total (4″ base + 2″ surface) for most residential driveways
  • Heavier vehicles or poor soil may require 8-12″ total

Depth Guide

Recommended depths per application.

Base:

  • 4″ Crusher Run / 304 Stone — most common
  • 3″ for stable, well-drained soil
  • 6″ for weak soil or heavy vehicle load

Surface:

  • 2″ #57 Stone — most common
  • 1.5″ for low-traffic areas
  • 3″ for heavier loads or softer material

Bag and Pallet Breakdown

How your material is packaged and priced.

Bag sizes:

  • 50 lb bags (most common for pickup truck loads)
  • 40 lb bags (sometimes available)
  • 25 kg bags (metric, ~55 lb)

Pallet config:

  • 40 bags per pallet (50 lb bags)
  • ~1 short ton (2,000 lb) = 40 bags
  • 1 metric tonne (2,205 lb) ≈ 44 bags (50 lb equivalent)

Example: 4 cubic yards of #57 stone (100 lb/ft³) = 108 ft³ = 10,800 lb ≈ 5.40 short tons ≈ 216 bags (50 lb) ≈ 6 pallets (5 full + 16 loose bags at 40 bags/pallet).

FAQ

How much gravel do I need for a driveway?
Depends on dimensions and depth. For a 20′ × 10′ driveway (200 ft²) with 4″ crusher run base (105 lb/ft³) + 2″ #57 stone surface (100 lb/ft³): ~3.70 cubic yards total (~3.50 short tons base + 1.67 short tons surface, before allowances).
What's the best gravel for a driveway base?
Crusher Run or 304 Stone — compacts densely, provides stable foundation and frost protection.
How many tons of gravel per square foot?
At 4″ depth (105 lb/ft³): ~1.75 short tons per 100 ft². At 2″ depth (100 lb/ft³): ~0.83 short tons per 100 ft². Varies with material density and compaction.
How many bags of gravel equal a cubic yard?
Approx. 54 bags of 50 lb gravel per cubic yard (for density ~100 lb/ft³).
Do I need a permit for a gravel driveway?
Check with your local municipality. Some areas require permits for driveways over a certain size or that affect drainage.
